Proxy Front

A first-person RTS. You start as one worker with a drill. Your base builds an army, your army tells you its plans, and you can jump into any unit you own.

An original world. The play pattern is classic 90s base-building RTS with a human industrial side and a psionic alien side. The factions, units, names and art are our own: the Ironhold Charter and the Veyl Chorus. "Proxy Front" is a working title.

Starting as a worker

What is happening

You are a Rigger, 1:42 into the match. Build mode is open and a Bunkhouse blueprint follows your crosshair. AI Riggers are mining the ore field on the right.

Build from eye level

2 opens the build list. The hologram snaps to the ground where you look. Place it, then hold the drill on it to weld it up.

The Quartermaster

The macro AI runs the chosen playbook and says what it wants next. You can follow its lead, overrule it, or switch it off.

Jump: take control of any unit

Hold Tab

The world dims and the game becomes a top-down RTS for a moment. Every squad shows what it is doing right now. Release on one and you are inside it.

Nothing stops when you leave

The body you leave carries on with its job. The squad you join follows you as its new lead.

Fast paths

F jumps into the friendly you are looking at. Q returns to your last body. A three second cooldown stops jump-dodging.

The army says its plan out loud

What is happening

You are a Rigger at the south choke. Alpha squad is about to attack and its lead asks you for a Watchpost. The request appears as a ghost blueprint. The target is marked in the distance.

One-key replies

Z affirm, X hold, C change play, V on me. Silence counts as yes after five seconds, so the army never stalls.

Playbooks

Squads follow standard RTS strategies: rush, fast expand, timing push, harassment, contain and more. The bar at the top shows which phase the current play is in.

The other side: a Veyl Cantor in a fight

A different game in your hands

Veyl units are few, tough and ability-driven. The Cantor is a melee fighter with a dash and a shockwave. Its Hush barrier recharges out of combat.

A different HUD

Ironhold's screen is riveted steel and pixel type. Veyl's is thin curved glass. Changing faction changes how the whole screen feels.

Skill is the bonus

The unit you control has the same stats as an AI one. You make it better by aiming, flanking and timing abilities, like striking as the Warden's dome absorbs fire.

Art direction: ink and flat colour

Shading recipe

Two flat tones per surface with a hard edge, heavy ink outlines, a thin rim light, and team colour on shoulders and stripes.

Nods to 90s RTS games

  • A heavy bevelled console with a framed, scanlined unit portrait
  • Pixel type for labels and numbers
  • Green selection rings on the tactical map
  • A short acknowledgement line when you jump into a unit

Decisions needed before building

  1. Name. Is "Proxy Front" fine as a working title?
  2. Platform. Browser first with three.js (recommended: shareable by link, nothing to install), or a desktop engine such as Godot?
  3. Player power. The unit you control gets no stat bonus in this design. Should it be noticeably stronger instead?
  4. Default autonomy. Should the AI run the economy by default, or should new players build by hand first?
  5. Match length. The target is 12 to 20 minutes. Shorter?
